## 2026-07-24 panel ceiling analysis (pre-Attempt-2 design instrument)

Before investing in the Attempt-2 judge swap, I quantified - with no GPU - whether the single-shot 28-row holdout design can certify even an upgraded panel under the registered rate floors. The registered ceiling analysis replayed rounds 3–4 through the exact predicate-level panel aggregation and reproduced both reports gate-for-gate before any estimate was trusted. The 2-of-3 row-correctness approximation used by the simulator matched the exact predicate replay on all 56 fresh rows with zero mismatches.

Fresh-data evidence (v19+v20, 56 adjudicated rows): panel errors are unsafe 1/12, over-verification 0/12, ambiguity 0/7, passing 0/24, panel parse 55/56. Per judge on the gate-relevant criterion: granite 1/56 (an ambiguity miss in round 3 - its only fresh error is not binary), phi 3/55 plus the round-3 parse failure, falcon 3/56; the only correlated error is the round-4 unsafe miss shared by phi and falcon.

With Jeffreys priors and 20,000 posterior-predictive draws per scenario through the production `holdout_gate_metrics`: the current panel passes a fresh 28-row holdout with probability ~0.12. A panel whose third seat is granite-quality reaches only ~0.40 (independent errors) / ~0.28 (correlated with falcon at the observed rate). Four gates behave as zero-miss floors at every simulated size (parse over 3 judges x all rows; unsafe 6-12; over-verification 6-12; ambiguity 4-8), so raising n LOWERS the pass probability - n=56 roughly halves it. The binding constraint is not judge accuracy but the conjunction of zero-miss floors: parse alone fails 0.45-0.65 of simulated holdouts under honest posteriors on 0-1 observed failures per judge. Count-slack floors (at most one miss where the denominator is at least 10) lift the upgraded panel to ~0.61/0.41 at n=28; extending the same slack to the ambiguity gate gives ~0.77/0.63. Discrimination (upgraded over current) is 2.4-3.4x, so the instrument does distinguish panels; its absolute pass probability is simply miscalibrated against the floors' intent for any panel.

Decision consequence: holdout #5 must be pre-registered with a floor-structure decision made before any row is drawn (never post-hoc), and the size curve says larger n alone makes certification harder, not easier. The scenario grid, priors, seeds, and input hashes are recorded in `W07_Panel_Ceiling_Analysis.json`; the design choice is deferred to the registration checkpoint after the Phi-4 (14B) development screen and recalibration produce real evidence for the new seat.
